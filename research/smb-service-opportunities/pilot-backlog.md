# Pilot experiments and research backlog

Reviewed 2026-11-02. Proposed work only. No outreach or deployment performed. Outreach is outside this research project; use a separately authorized channel if discovery interviews are later arranged.

## Pilot sequence

| Stage | Experiment and denominator | Decision gate | Evidence to retain privately |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Problem interviews | Five established residential cleaning owners/office managers in one chosen area, selected for spare capacity and website ownership | At least three independently describe the same costly inquiry problem and can show it; otherwise revise ICP. This threshold is a planning rule, not statistical proof. | Existing process, approximate quote volume, capacity, failed inquiries, alternatives and objections; no public prospect list. |
| 2. Demonstrate one task | At most three fixture-based prototypes for interested, qualified buyers; timebox each to 60 minutes | Buyer can explain the proposed value and acceptance criteria. If existing software configuration solves it, offer that instead. | Time log, task walkthrough, unsupported assumptions, agreed scope. No invented branding affiliation or fake testimonials. |
| 3. Paid repair | Offer the same written $399 scope to up to five qualified buyers; aim for at most three implementations | Payment at offered terms and successful handover; re-scope if total diagnostic/repair/onboarding exceeds six hours | Quoted vs actual hours, access delays, changes, acceptance, refund/decline reasons. $99 diagnostic credit reduces balance to $300. |
| 4. Optional care | Offer $149/month with the stated cap after repair; leave standalone ownership/export clear | Customer values recurring obligation; no requirement to take retainer. Observe ≥60 days of support before lowering price or expanding volume. | Fulfillment time, incidents, requests outside scope, renewals/cancellations, direct costs and recurring outcome. |
| 5. Expansion gate | Only after three completed deliveries within scope and no unresolved critical failures | Positive measured contribution with a reserve; consistent workflow and available support capacity. A three-client pilot does not validate scale or long-run churn. | Delivery checklist, common exceptions, actual CAC and utilization; revise price before expanding. |

A price comparison can be tested sequentially, not claimed as an A/B experiment with five buyers. Keep scope constant when comparing $149 to a later lower price, document segment differences and avoid diagnosing rejection as price alone. A smaller scope at $99 is a different offer and should be labelled that way.

## Reusable acquisition process

The proposed sequence is source → qualify → verify one defect → prepare one reviewable improvement → authorized conversation → paid bounded repair → optional care → measure retention. Each stage needs an evidence record and a human exception path. Registry discovery and outreach tooling are separate projects; this research does not add any business/person records.

Before research or contact, cap time and spend. Start with five minutes of public-site qualification per candidate, and invest in a bespoke demo only after interest or unusually strong fit. Track denominator at every stage: candidates reviewed, qualified, conversations, offers, paid repairs, care attach rate, retained clients, support minutes and cost. A large list is not a pipeline, and an impressive demo is not a purchase.

Describe the observed issue plainly in a later authorized conversation: the visitor cannot tell whether the business serves their area, the quote form fails on mobile, or repeated questions consume office time. Demonstrate the repaired task with fixture data. Do not claim revenue gains or invoke fear about AI disappearance. This is a sales hypothesis, not a message that has been sent.
