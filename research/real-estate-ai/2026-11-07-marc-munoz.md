## Claim ledger and important qualifications

**C1 — QBI and LLC framing.** The permanent federal extension is supported by the Pennsylvania Department of Revenue's summary of the 2025 law [S1]. IRS guidance describes an **up-to-20%** deduction for eligible qualified business income, not an automatic benefit of forming an LLC. Sole proprietors and eligible pass-through owners may qualify; employee wages and C-corporation income do not. QBI is net qualifying income, with taxable-income and other limitations [S2]. The source's flat dollar illustrations should not be adopted as a personal result.

**C2 — Bonus depreciation and the selected fourth slide.** The IRS confirms permanent 100% additional first-year depreciation for eligible property under the amended law [S3]. That does not mean 100% of a rental house, or a fixed 20–40% of every property's market value, is deductible. IRS Publication 527 distinguishes qualifying shorter-life assets from residential rental buildings, generally depreciated over 27.5 years under GDS; land is not depreciable. Acquisition, placed-in-service dates, basis and asset classification matter [S4]. The post's percentage range is not validated by a property-specific cost-segregation study.

**C3 — Stacking the deductions.** Rental losses are generally passive, with exceptions and limits; they do not automatically offset unrelated active business earnings [S5]. QBI is calculated from qualifying net items and is itself subject to limitations [S2]. Consequently, $100,000 minus $20,000 minus $30,000 is arithmetic, not a verified tax computation. Do not assume the two deductions are independent, fully usable in the same year, or a reduction in tax dollar-for-dollar.

**C4 — 1031 and inheritance.** Section 1031 applies to qualifying investment/business real property, not property held primarily for sale. Reinvesting proceeds alone is insufficient; deferred exchanges have identification/completion rules, including the usual 45-day identification window and completion by the earlier of 180 days or the applicable return deadline [S6, S7]. Contract-flipping income is not made tax-deferred merely by later buying a rental. Inherited property generally receives a fair-market-value basis under the applicable rules, and a later sale above basis can produce taxable gain [S8]. These mechanisms do not establish that all taxes disappear.

**C5 — Broad lifestyle write-offs.** Ordinary, necessary business costs and personal spending are not interchangeable. The IRS explicitly says personal expenses cannot simply be written off as business expenses [S9]. An LLC does not transform personal consumption into a business deduction.

**C6 — Deals, deposits and rental income.** Treat every property price, deposit, margin, rent and annual-income figure above as an unverified source scenario. The reviewed post does not establish assignability of a particular government sale, the availability of a qualified buyer, net operating income, rehabilitation cost, title condition or local program eligibility. Auction deposits and deadlines, assignment restrictions, licensing/disclosure rules, financing, tenancy and housing-program requirements must be checked for the specific jurisdiction and property. No universal legal conclusion is made here.

## Mined ideas: original adaptations

### RE-AI-001 — Evidence-first property opportunity screen

- **Category/state:** discovery and diagnosis; proposed research experiment.
- **Target task/buyer:** a small investor or licensed operator deciding whether a public listing merits professional diligence.
- **Evidence:** slides 6–8 suggest a sourcing-to-deal workflow. Demand, supply quality and willingness to pay remain unknown.
- **Original adaptation:** collect official listing links, dates, sale mechanism, deposit/payment terms, stated assignment restrictions and missing evidence into a reviewable worksheet. Never invent a valuation or treat an auction minimum as an attainable purchase price.
- **Alternative/value hypothesis:** compare with manual auction pages and a spreadsheet; value would be faster identification of disqualifying conditions, not a promised profit.
- **Inputs/cost drivers:** permitted public listings, recorded sale terms, manual review time, authorized data licenses and professional diligence.
- **Risks/human role:** stale listings, liens, title defects, nonassignable agreements, lost deposits and local licensing rules. A human verifies terms and obtains legal/title advice before action.
- **Smallest safe test:** manually review five public listings in one named jurisdiction without contacting owners, bidding or paying. Require a source and retrieval date for every substantive field, mark unknowns, and measure time and error rate against manual review. Stop if use rights or material terms cannot be established.

### RE-AI-002 — CPA-ready tax-claim worksheet

- **Category/state:** compliance/risk; proposed research experiment.
- **Target task/buyer:** a business owner or bookkeeping team preparing a factual question set for a CPA.
- **Evidence:** slides 2–5 and 9 combine distinct rules into simple examples; C1–C5 identify important conditions.
- **Original adaptation:** map each marketing claim to the relevant rule, required facts, evidence gaps and professional reviewer. Produce questions, not eligibility determinations or a recommended entity/filing position.
- **Alternative/value hypothesis:** a normal CPA intake checklist; potential value is clearer preparation and fewer missing facts. No demand or savings are established.
- **Inputs/cost drivers:** current official guidance, synthetic scenarios, licensed reviewer time and securely supplied client facts only in a separately authorized private workflow.
- **Risks/human role:** stale law, state nonconformity, interactions between deductions and sensitive financial data. A CPA owns any real calculation or advice.
- **Smallest safe test:** three synthetic cases: sole proprietor, active flipping business plus passive rental, and rental with land/building/components. Pass only if each case flags eligibility, basis, income-limit and loss-use questions and never outputs a guaranteed deduction. Any professional review requires separate authorization before contact.

### RE-AI-003 — Rental cash-flow and downside worksheet

- **Category/state:** operations and diagnosis; proposed research experiment.
- **Target task/buyer:** a small rental operator comparing an acquisition with maintaining cash or passing on the deal.
- **Evidence:** slide 8 quotes purchase and gross rent figures but does not establish operating economics.
- **Original adaptation:** separate purchase, closing, rehabilitation, vacancy, maintenance, management, taxes, insurance, debt service and cash reserves from gross rent. Keep tax effects separate from operating cash flow.
- **Alternative/value hypothesis:** a conventional underwriting spreadsheet; potential value is transparent assumptions and easier downside comparison.
- **Inputs/cost drivers:** synthetic cases first; later only authorized property documents, local program rules, verified estimates and reviewer time.
- **Risks/human role:** unrealistic rents, repair surprises, nonpayment, program/inspection conditions and fair-housing obligations. No tenant scoring, eligibility decision or automated outreach.
- **Smallest safe test:** one synthetic base/downside pair with every assumption labeled. Require visibly distinct gross income, operating income and post-debt cash flow; missing costs must remain unknown rather than silently zero. No investment recommendation follows.
